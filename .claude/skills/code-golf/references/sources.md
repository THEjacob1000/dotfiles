# Sources and provenance

This is independently authored guidance for reducing maintenance burden while preserving supported behavior and readability. It is informed by [Dietrich Gebert's Ponytail](https://github.com/DietrichGebert/ponytail), specifically its [core skill](https://github.com/DietrichGebert/ponytail/blob/main/.openclaw/skills/ponytail/SKILL.md). Ponytail's useful starting questions are whether the work is needed and whether existing code, standard libraries, native platform features, or installed dependencies already do it. This skill extends that starting point with a scoped reduction workflow, explicit keep-versus-delete decisions, contract and reachability proof, test-retirement rules, language-specific semantic hazards, and a hard readability gate. It does not copy Ponytail's text, examples, persona, intensity modes, or output rules, and does not treat shortest source or a one-line solution as the objective.

Any benchmark numbers reported by the upstream author are their own claims, not measurements of this skill; no success, speed, token, or line-count improvement is claimed here without an actual comparison.

## Claim map

- [Martin Fowler, Definition of Refactoring](https://martinfowler.com/bliki/DefinitionOfRefactoring.html) defines internal restructuring by preserved observable behavior and improved comprehensibility/change cost. That supports this skill's distinction between semantic reduction and text shortening. It does not prove a particular edit safe.
- [Martin Fowler, Workflows of Refactoring](https://martinfowler.com/articles/workflowsOfRefactoring/fallback.html) distinguishes behavior-preserving small steps from adding functionality and describes opportunistic, preparatory, and planned work. That informs the staged workflow in [workflow](workflow.md), not an assumption that a green suite proves equivalence for every external consumer.
- [Google Testing Blog, Too Many Tests](https://testing.googleblog.com/2008/02/in-movie-amadeus-austrian-emperor.html), [Test Sizes](https://testing.googleblog.com/2010/12/test-sizes.html), and [Just Say No to More End-to-End Tests](https://testing.googleblog.com/2015/04/just-say-no-to-more-end-to-end-tests.html) discuss test value, size, and cost tradeoffs. Our per-assertion obligation ledger in [proof and tests](proof-and-tests.md) is a local decision rule derived from those concerns, not a claim that the articles authorize deleting integration or boundary tests. The surviving witness must still detect a plausible regression, and project policy can forbid retirement.
- Compiler and runtime limits, container semantics, and version-sensitive transformations are sourced in the respective [Rust](rust.md), [TypeScript/JavaScript](typescript-javascript.md), and [Go](go.md) guides beside their claims. General refactoring advice cannot establish language-specific safety; check the applicable compiler, runtime, build variants, and supported versions.
